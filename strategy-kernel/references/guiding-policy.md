# Guiding Policy

Use guiding policy to define the simplest viable approach for addressing the
diagnosis.

The guiding policy is a map: a guide for how to move through the situation. It
is not a slogan, a mood, a vision statement, or a task list.

It should read like a map drawn after inspecting the wreckage: concrete,
constraint-aware, and useful for deciding what not to do.

## Write The Policy

Write the guiding policy as a bullet-pointed list. Each bullet should give a
usable decision guide:

- Where to focus first, and why that focus addresses the diagnosis.
- What constraints must shape decisions.
- What tradeoffs should guide design choices.
- What to avoid because it would distract from the strategy.
- How to choose between plausible options.

Avoid:

- Listing implementation tasks.
- Choosing implementation details before the diagnosis supports them.
- Expanding scope beyond the stated goal without evidence.
- Abstract business language, generic advice, or filler.
- Policies that sound good but do not guide a real decision.

## Quality Bar

The guiding policy should make the coherent actions feel inevitable. If multiple
unrelated action plans could follow from the policy, tighten the policy until it
gives clearer direction.

Every bullet must pass this test: "Would this help someone decide what to do or
not do next?" If not, remove it.
